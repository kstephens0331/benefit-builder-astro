## How a cafeteria plan works here

When an employee makes a pre-tax election, the chosen amount is set aside before income tax is calculated, so the benefit is funded from gross pay rather than take-home pay. Colorado applies a flat 4.4% state income tax with no standard deduction, so the pre-tax treatment reaches Centennial wages from the first dollar. As a routine and factual matter, pre-tax elections also lower the wages subject to payroll tax, which modestly reduces an employer's payroll cost per enrolled worker, but the reason Centennial employers adopt a plan is the benefit it puts in front of their people. ## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan offers a defined menu of qualified benefits. The common items are health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts; health savings account contributions when paired with a qualifying high-deductible plan; and dependent care assistance. Long-term care insurance generally cannot be funded pre-tax inside a Section 125 plan. The exact menu an employer offers is set in the plan document.
