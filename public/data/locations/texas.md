## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Texas employers

Texas is the second most populous state in the country and one of its largest job markets, with major economic centers in Houston, Dallas-Fort Worth, San Antonio, Austin, and El Paso. From the Gulf Coast energy and petrochemical corridor to the medical campuses of the Texas Medical Center, the tech and government payrolls of Central Texas, and the cross-border trade that moves through Laredo and the Rio Grande Valley, the state's employers compete constantly for skilled and hourly workers alike. A Section 125 cafeteria plan gives those employers a concrete way to strengthen their benefits package and stand out as a place people want to work.

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through their regular payroll. For the employee, that means real medical, dental, vision, and account-based benefits funded out of each paycheck on more favorable terms than buying the same coverage on their own. For the employer, offering those pre-tax benefits is a recruiting and retention tool that does not require inflating gross wages. In a state where construction crews, hospital systems, and distribution centers are all hiring at once, a stronger benefits menu is often what tips a candidate's decision.

## Why the benefits matter across Texas industries

Texas employers fall into a handful of sectors that all lean on benefits to keep people. Public and private schools across the state's nearly 1,200 districts run large, benefits-conscious payrolls and stretch every dollar of an education budget. Healthcare and senior care providers, from the Texas Medical Center in Houston to clinics and nursing facilities statewide, staff around the clock and need benefits that retain nurses and aides. Energy, manufacturing, and the skilled trades along the Gulf Coast and in the metros employ large hourly workforces that value coverage they can pay for through payroll. Trucking, logistics, and warehousing, especially along the I-35 trade corridor and the border ports, run driver-heavy rosters where benefits help reduce turnover. Professional services firms in accounting, law, engineering, and consulting use a richer pre-tax menu to attract experienced staff.

For any of these employers, the appeal is the same: a [Section 125 plan](/services/employers) lets the team access health, dental, vision, and account-based benefits on a pre-tax basis, which makes the same paycheck go further and makes the job offer more competitive. ## How a cafeteria plan works here

When an employee makes a pre-tax election, the chosen amount is set aside before income tax is calculated, so the benefit is funded from gross pay rather than take-home pay. Because Texas has no state income tax, there is no state withholding layer to work around. As a routine and factual matter, pre-tax elections also lower the wages subject to payroll tax, which modestly reduces an employer's payroll cost per enrolled worker, but the reason most Texas employers adopt a plan is the benefit it puts in front of their people.

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan offers a defined menu of qualified benefits. The common items are health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts; health savings account contributions; and dependent care assistance. Long-term care insurance generally cannot be funded pre-tax inside a Section 125 plan, which is a frequent point of confusion. The exact menu an employer offers is set in the plan document.
