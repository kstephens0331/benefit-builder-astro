A Section 125 cafeteria plan gives Missouri employers a practical way to put pre-tax benefits in front of W-2 employees, which makes a job offer more competitive and helps keep good people on the payroll. The plan lets workers pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, so the same paycheck stretches further and the employer has a real recruiting and retention tool without rewriting its pay scale. That matters across the breadth of Missouri's economy, from the metros down to the small towns of the Bootheel.

## How a Section 125 plan works in Missouri

A cafeteria plan lets an employee make a qualified election, and that elected amount is removed from the wages used to calculate payroll taxes before those taxes are figured. Because Missouri uses a flat state income tax of 4.7 percent with a standard deduction in line with the federal figure, pre-tax elections are simple to administer here. Federal income tax is graduated, and FICA is a flat 7.65 percent split as Social Security at 6.2 percent up to the annual wage base plus Medicare at 1.45 percent. As a rolled-in matter of fact, because the qualified election sits outside FICA wages, the employer's matching payroll cost on those dollars is lower as well. That effect is a byproduct, not the reason to offer the plan. You can model figures for a specific roster on the [plan calculator](/savings-calculator).

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A Section 125 plan can shelter a defined set of qualified benefits. These include health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts; health savings account contributions; and dependent care assistance. Long-term care insurance generally does not qualify to run through a cafeteria plan. Each category has its own rules on limits and eligibility, so the elections should be set up against current guidance.
