## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Michigan employers

Michigan runs on payrolls that have to compete hard for skilled people, and a Section 125 cafeteria plan gives employers here a practical way to widen what those payrolls offer. The plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which stretches their paychecks further, and it gives an employer a recruiting and retention tool that costs far less to stand up than a richer health plan or across-the-board raises. In a state where the auto sector, healthcare systems, and a growing professional and tech base all chase the same workers, the ability to put a real pre-tax benefit menu in front of a candidate matters.

## Who employs people in Michigan

Michigan's economy is anchored by motor vehicle and parts manufacturing. The Detroit metro -- Wayne, Oakland, and Macomb counties -- is home to the domestic automakers and a deep tier of suppliers, tooling shops, and engineering firms in places like Dearborn, Warren, Auburn Hills, and Troy. That manufacturing base reaches well beyond Detroit: Grand Rapids and the West Michigan furniture and food-processing corridor, the auto and pharmaceutical employers around Kalamazoo and Battle Creek, and the cluster of suppliers ringing Flint and Lansing all run substantial production and skilled-trades payrolls.

Beyond the shop floor, healthcare is one of the state's largest employers, with major hospital systems and physician groups across the Detroit, Grand Rapids, and Ann Arbor markets. Ann Arbor and East Lansing add a strong university and research presence, which feeds a professional, life-sciences, and technology workforce. Agriculture and food processing run through the central and western counties, and tourism and seasonal hospitality carry the economies of northern Michigan and the Upper Peninsula. Each of these sectors -- manufacturing, healthcare, professional services, education, and hospitality -- has employers who use a [Section 125 plan](/services/employers) to round out what they offer their people.

## Why the plan fits Michigan workforces

Michigan's signature challenge is competition for skilled and hourly workers alike. Auto suppliers and tooling shops compete with the automakers for machinists, technicians, and engineers. Hospitals and clinics compete statewide for nurses and clinical staff. A cafeteria plan helps on both fronts: it lets a smaller supplier or a regional clinic offer a benefits package that reads as competitive against a much larger employer, without the cost of matching that employer dollar for dollar. Employees get to set aside money pre-tax for health, dental, and vision premiums, medical and dependent-care expenses, and other qualified benefits, so their take-home pay goes further on costs they were already facing.

For Michigan's hourly and shift-based payrolls, the structure is forgiving. Manufacturing rosters with steady pay periods and even seasonal hospitality operations in the north can apply pre-tax deductions consistently once the plan is set up, because the elections are tied to each employee's choices rather than to a fixed schedule. ## A note on cost

There is a payroll-cost angle worth stating plainly: because qualified pre-tax elections come out before FICA is calculated, an employer's matching FICA falls on the dollars that run through the plan, which helps offset the cost of offering it. That is a real effect, but it is a byproduct, not the reason to adopt a plan. The reason is the benefit itself -- giving employees more usable income and giving the employer a stronger offer in a tight labor market.

## What can run pre-tax

A Section 125 plan for a Michigan employer can carry health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care FSAs; health savings account contributions made through the plan; and dependent care assistance. Some benefits do not qualify -- long-term care insurance generally cannot be offered pre-tax through a cafeteria plan -- so building the menu correctly is part of keeping every election eligible. Getting that menu right is one of the things a third party administrator handles.
