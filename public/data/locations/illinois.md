## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Illinois employers

Illinois is one of the most economically diverse states in the country, and that diversity shapes how employers compete for workers. From the corporate towers and logistics hubs of metropolitan Chicago to the grain elevators and farm cooperatives of the central prairie, employers across the state are fighting the same battle: attracting and keeping good people without blowing up payroll. A Section 125 cafeteria plan helps them do exactly that, by letting employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars so the same paycheck stretches further and the benefits package looks more competitive. Benefit Builder administers these plans for employers statewide. See [how plans work for employers](/services/employers) for the full picture.

## The Illinois economy we serve

Illinois has a layered economy, and the value of a strong benefits package shows up differently in each layer.

The Chicago metro area -- Cook County plus the collar counties of DuPage, Lake, Will, Kane, and McHenry -- is the financial, professional services, and corporate center of the Midwest. It is home to derivatives and futures trading, major banks and insurance carriers, advertising and consulting firms, and a dense cluster of corporate headquarters in suburbs like Schaumburg, Naperville, and Deerfield. White-collar employers here compete hard on benefits, and a [cafeteria plan](/industries) lets them offer pre-tax health, dental, and dependent care options that professional candidates expect.

Logistics and freight are an Illinois signature. Chicago is the busiest rail interchange in North America, and Will County's intermodal yards around Joliet and Elwood move an enormous share of the nation's containers. Trucking, warehousing, and distribution employers across Will, DuPage, and downstate corridors run large hourly workforces where take-home pay drives retention. Pre-tax benefits give those workers more usable income from the same wage.

Manufacturing remains deeply rooted statewide -- heavy equipment and machinery around Peoria, food processing, fabricated metals, and the chemical and refining corridor near Joliet and the Metro East counties across from St. Louis. The Rockford region in Winnebago County is a long-standing center for aerospace components and precision machining. These employers run shift-based payrolls where a clear, pre-tax benefits structure helps hold experienced tradespeople.

Healthcare is among the largest employer sectors in nearly every Illinois market. Chicago's academic medical centers, regional hospital systems in Springfield, Peoria, Rockford, and the Quad Cities, plus the dense network of clinics, dental offices, and long-term care facilities downstate all employ large, benefits-sensitive staffs. For practices and facilities, see [our work with healthcare employers](/industries).

Agriculture and agribusiness anchor central and southern Illinois. The state is a national leader in corn and soybean production, and counties like McLean, Champaign, Sangamon, and the surrounding prairie support grain handlers, seed and equipment dealers, ethanol producers, and farm cooperatives. These employers often run lean back offices, which is precisely where outside administration earns its keep.

Education and public institutions matter too. The University of Illinois system, with its flagship in Champaign-Urbana, along with state government in Springfield and a broad base of school districts, employs benefits-eligible staff across the state.

## Why employers here choose pre-tax benefits

The common thread across these sectors is competition for labor. A Section 125 plan lets an Illinois employer offer qualified pre-tax benefits -- health, dental, and vision premiums, health and dependent care FSAs, and HSA contributions when paired with a qualifying high-deductible plan -- so employees get more value from each paycheck and the employer looks stronger against the business down the road. Because qualified elections come out before payroll taxes are calculated, the employer's payroll costs on those amounts are modestly lower as well, which is a real but secondary effect rather than the point of the plan.

For a downstate dental practice, a Joliet warehouse operator, or a Naperville professional firm, the appeal is the same: a benefits package that helps recruit and retain without the administrative burden of running it in-house.
