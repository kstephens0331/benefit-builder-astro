## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Colorado employers

Colorado runs on a mix of high-skill and high-turnover industries that all compete hard for workers, and a benefits package is often what decides where someone takes a job. The Denver metro anchors the state's economy along the Front Range, where aerospace, software, telecommunications, and financial services cluster from Boulder through Denver and down to Colorado Springs. Outside the metro corridor, Colorado's economy runs on healthcare systems, ski and mountain resort tourism, agriculture on the Eastern Plains, energy production in the Denver-Julesburg and Piceance basins, and a heavy federal and military presence. A Section 125 cafeteria plan gives all of these employers a concrete way to make their benefits go further and to compete for the people they need.

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through their regular payroll. For the employee, that means medical, dental, vision, and account-based benefits funded out of each paycheck on better terms than buying the same coverage on their own. For the employer, offering those pre-tax benefits is a recruiting and retention tool that does not require raising gross wages. In a state where ski resorts, hospital systems, defense contractors, and tech startups are all hiring at the same time, a stronger benefits menu is frequently what closes the deal with a candidate.

## How a cafeteria plan works here

When an employee makes a pre-tax election, the chosen amount is set aside before income tax is calculated, so the benefit is funded from gross pay rather than take-home pay. Colorado applies a flat 4.4% state income tax with no standard deduction, so a pre-tax election reaches the first dollar of wages just as it reaches the rest. As a routine and factual matter, pre-tax elections also lower the wages subject to payroll tax, which modestly reduces an employer's payroll cost per enrolled worker, but the reason most Colorado employers adopt a plan is the benefit it puts in front of their people.

## What benefits can run pre-tax

A cafeteria plan offers a defined menu of qualified benefits. The common items are health, dental, and vision insurance premiums; health flexible spending accounts and dependent care flexible spending accounts; health savings account contributions when paired with a qualifying high-deductible plan; and dependent care assistance. Long-term care insurance generally cannot be funded pre-tax inside a Section 125 plan, which is a frequent point of confusion. The exact menu an employer offers is set in the plan document.
