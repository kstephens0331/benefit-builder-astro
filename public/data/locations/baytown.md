## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Baytown employers

Baytown sits on the northern shore of Galveston Bay in eastern Harris County, along the industrial heart of the Houston Ship Channel. It is one of the most concentrated petrochemical and refining hubs in the country, home to one of the largest refining and chemical complexes in the United States and a dense cluster of plants, contractors, and industrial service firms. Around that industrial core sit healthcare providers, retailers, and a base of small businesses serving the local population. The workforce spans hourly plant and craft labor and salaried office roles alike, and a Section 125 cafeteria plan gives Baytown employers a real way to make their benefits package more attractive in a competitive industrial labor market.

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through payroll. For the worker, that means medical, dental, vision, and account-based benefits funded on more favorable terms than buying coverage alone. For a Baytown employer, offering those pre-tax benefits is a recruiting and retention tool that strengthens the job offer without raising gross wages, which matters when plants, contractors, and turnaround crews are all hiring from the same pool.

## How a cafeteria plan works here

When an employee makes a pre-tax election, the amount is set aside before income tax is calculated, so the benefit is funded from gross pay. Because Texas has no state income tax, there is no state withholding layer to factor in. As a routine matter, those pre-tax elections also lower the wages subject to payroll tax, which modestly trims an employer's payroll cost per enrolled worker, but the reason Baytown employers adopt a plan is the benefit it delivers to their workforce.
