## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Houston employers

Houston is the seat of Harris County and the largest city in Texas, anchoring one of the most diverse regional economies in the country. It is the energy capital of the United States, home to the world's largest medical complex in the Texas Medical Center, a major port and petrochemical corridor along the Houston Ship Channel, and a sprawling base of manufacturers, logistics operators, and professional firms. Beneath those headline industries sits a broad base of small and mid-size businesses. Across all of it, employers compete hard for skilled and hourly workers, and a Section 125 cafeteria plan gives them a real way to make their benefits package more attractive.

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through their regular payroll. For the worker, that means medical, dental, vision, and account-based benefits funded on more favorable terms than buying coverage individually. For a Houston employer, offering those pre-tax benefits is a recruiting and retention tool that strengthens the job offer without inflating gross wages, which matters whether the company runs a single office inside the Loop or staffs hundreds of workers across multiple sites.

## Benefits scale for large and small Houston workforces

A cafeteria plan works from the first enrolled employee forward, so it fits a ten-person professional services firm and a thousand-person hospital system equally well. A large Houston workforce gives more employees access to pre-tax benefits at once, while a small office gets the same per-employee value over a smaller base. There is no minimum size at which the plan suddenly starts working.

What separates a plan that delivers from one that underperforms is participation, and participation depends on two things being handled well: payroll coordination and employee education. Deductions have to be set up correctly and synced with each pay run, and employees have to understand what the plan does for them before they will elect into it. Benefit Builder handles both, so the benefits the plan is capable of offering actually reach the workforce. As a routine matter, those pre-tax elections also reduce the wages subject to payroll tax, which modestly lowers an employer's payroll cost per enrolled worker.
