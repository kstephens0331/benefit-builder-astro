## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Farmington employers

Farmington is the commercial and medical hub of San Juan County and the largest city in the Four Corners region of northwest New Mexico, where New Mexico meets Arizona, Colorado, and Utah. It serves a trade area that reaches far beyond its own city limits, drawing workers and shoppers from Aztec, Bloomfield, Kirtland, and the Navajo Nation communities to the west and south. For the employers who run those payrolls, a [Section 125 cafeteria plan](/services/employers) is a way to offer competitive, pre-tax benefits and hold on to good people in a labor market where the next job is often a long drive away.

A cafeteria plan lets an employee pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, so the same wage supports more usable coverage. For a Farmington employer, that is a recruiting and retention advantage that does not require raising base pay, and because the election reduces the wage base, the employer's matching FICA on participating wages drops as a rolled-in effect rather than the selling point.

## The Farmington and San Juan County economy

Farmington's economy was built on the San Juan Basin, one of the largest natural gas fields in the country, and energy remains central to the area's employer mix. Oil and gas operators, drilling and field service companies, pipeline and gathering firms, and the trades that support them employ crews across the basin. The region has also navigated the wind-down of coal generation, including the closure of the San Juan Generating Station, which has pushed local leaders toward economic diversification. That backdrop makes stable, portable benefits especially valuable to the energy and trades employers still hiring here.

Beyond energy, several sectors anchor local payrolls:

- **Healthcare.** San Juan Regional Medical Center and the surrounding clinics, specialty practices, and care facilities make healthcare one of Farmington's largest and steadiest employers, serving the entire Four Corners.
- **Retail and regional trade.** As the retail center for a wide rural and reservation trade area, Farmington supports a large base of retail, hospitality, automotive, and service employers along its commercial corridors.
- **Education and public sector.** Farmington, Aztec, Bloomfield, and Central Consolidated school districts, along with San Juan College and city and county government, run benefits-eligible payrolls across the county.
- **Tribal and community employers.** The nearby Navajo Nation, including communities such as Shiprock, operates government, healthcare, education, and enterprise payrolls that employ much of the surrounding population.

## Why the plan fits these payrolls

These rosters mix hourly field and clinical staff, school and college employees, retail and service workers, and administrative teams, and most of these employers run lean back offices. A cafeteria plan gives a Farmington oilfield service company, a clinic, a school district, or a Navajo enterprise the same pre-tax benefit structure that larger employers use, without building benefits administration in house. The same logic carries into Aztec, Bloomfield, Kirtland, and Shiprock, which is why pre-tax benefits travel well across San Juan County and the wider Four Corners.
