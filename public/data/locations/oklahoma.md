## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Oklahoma employers

Oklahoma runs on a few large, payroll-heavy engines: aerospace and defense, energy, healthcare, agriculture and food processing, public education, and tribal government. A Section 125 cafeteria plan gives employers in each of those sectors a practical way to put real benefits in front of workers and to compete for the people they need to keep. Benefit Builder administers those plans for Oklahoma employers so the benefits land correctly and the paperwork stays handled.

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through payroll. The employer offers a structured menu of benefits, the employee elects what fits their life, and the elections are documented and run through each pay cycle. The result is a stronger, more flexible benefits package that helps an employer recruit and retain without simply raising wages across the board.

## How a cafeteria plan fits Oklahoma's main industries

Aerospace and defense is the state's second-largest industry. Tinker Air Force Base in Oklahoma City is the largest single-site employer in Oklahoma, and the Greater Oklahoma City region hosts dozens of aircraft maintenance, repair, and overhaul firms. In Tulsa, American Airlines operates one of the largest aircraft maintenance bases in the world. Contractors, suppliers, and MRO shops feeding that ecosystem compete hard for skilled technicians and engineers, and a well-structured benefits menu is part of how they win and keep that talent. See how we tailor plans by sector on our [industries](/industries) page.

Energy is the other pillar. Oklahoma's oil and gas operators, midstream companies, and the service firms around them in the Oklahoma City and Tulsa metros run payrolls that swing with field activity. Pre-tax benefits give those employers a stable, valued part of the compensation package even when the rest of the cost structure moves.

Healthcare is a large and growing employer statewide, from the hospital systems and medical research corridor in Oklahoma City and Tulsa to the rural clinics and critical-access hospitals that anchor smaller towns. These organizations carry big rosters and tight margins, and a cafeteria plan helps them offer competitive benefits to nurses, technicians, and support staff.

Agriculture, food processing, and manufacturing round out the picture across the rural counties, alongside a deep public sector. Oklahoma's school districts, colleges, and city and county governments employ tens of thousands of people, and tribal nations are major employers in their own right. Each of these runs large, steady payrolls where a documented pre-tax benefits program is exactly the kind of structure that fits.

## What benefits can run through the plan

A cafeteria plan can carry a range of common benefits on a pre-tax basis: health, dental, and vision premiums, health and dependent care flexible spending accounts, health savings account contributions, and dependent care assistance. Each one expands what an employer can offer without restructuring base pay. As one factual note, because qualified elections are taken before payroll taxes are figured, the employer's share of FICA does not apply to those dollars, which modestly lowers payroll cost. That effect is a byproduct of running benefits correctly, not the reason to offer the plan.
