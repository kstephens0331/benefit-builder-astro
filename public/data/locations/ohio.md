## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Ohio employers

Ohio runs on payrolls. From the auto and parts plants of the industrial north, to the research and aerospace corridor around Dayton, to the financial and insurance towers of Columbus, the state's employers compete for skilled people every day. A Section 125 cafeteria plan is a recruiting and retention tool built for that competition: it lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars, which raises their take-home pay without raising what the employer spends on wages. For Ohio companies trying to hold onto experienced line workers, nurses, drivers, and office staff, that is a benefit they can put in front of a candidate or a current employee right now.

## Ohio's economy and where a plan fits

Ohio is one of the most industrially diverse states in the country, and its metro areas each pull from different sectors.

- Greater Cleveland and the northeast lean on manufacturing, steel and metals, healthcare, and one of the country's largest concentrations of hospital and clinic employment anchored by the Cleveland Clinic and University Hospitals systems.
- Columbus and central Ohio run on insurance and financial services, state government, higher education around Ohio State, logistics, and a fast-growing tech and advanced-manufacturing base, including major semiconductor investment in Licking County.
- Cincinnati and the southwest combine consumer products, banking, aerospace, and a deep base of corporate headquarters straddling the Ohio River.
- Dayton and the Miami Valley center on aerospace and advanced manufacturing, with Wright-Patterson Air Force Base anchoring a large defense and research workforce. See our [Dayton page](/locations/dayton) for that metro specifically.
- The Toledo area builds glass and automotive components, while Akron retains its polymer and materials heritage, and Youngstown and the Mahoning Valley continue a manufacturing and logistics tradition.

These are wage-heavy, people-heavy operations spread across counties like Cuyahoga, Franklin, Hamilton, Montgomery, Summit, Lucas, Stark, and Butler. Manufacturers running production shifts, hospital and long-term care systems staffing around the clock, trucking and warehouse operations along the I-70 and I-71 corridors, and the small businesses that fill every Ohio downtown all share the same problem: keeping good employees in a tight labor market. ## What employees can elect pre-tax

Under a Section 125 plan, employees choose from a menu of qualified benefits and pay for them before tax is calculated. Common elections include health, dental, and vision insurance premiums, health care flexible spending accounts, dependent care assistance accounts, and health savings account contributions made through the cafeteria plan. For an Ohio nurse, machinist, or office manager, electing these benefits pre-tax means more money in each paycheck for the same coverage, which is exactly the kind of concrete improvement that helps an employer recruit and keep staff. Because those elections come out of pay before federal income tax, FICA, and Ohio state income tax are figured, the employer's share of FICA on participating wages is reduced as well, a routine byproduct of how the plan is built rather than its selling point.
