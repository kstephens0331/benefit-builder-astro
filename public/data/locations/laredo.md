## Section 125 cafeteria plans for Laredo employers

Laredo is the seat of Webb County, sitting on the Rio Grande at the United States and Mexico border. It is the busiest inland port in the country, and the World Trade International Bridge here handles an enormous share of the truck freight that crosses between the two nations. That trade drives the local economy: customs brokers, freight carriers, warehousing and distribution, healthcare, retail, and education all run deep payrolls in Laredo, with many of the largest employers staffing big hourly workforces year round. A Section 125 cafeteria plan gives those employers a real way to make their benefits package more competitive in a market where workers have plenty of options.

A cafeteria plan lets employees pay for qualified benefits with pre-tax dollars through payroll. For the worker, that means medical, dental, vision, and account-based benefits funded on more favorable terms than buying coverage alone, which hourly employees in particular feel directly in each paycheck. For a Laredo employer, offering those pre-tax benefits is a recruiting and retention tool that strengthens the job offer without raising gross wages.

## Why the benefits fit Laredo's trade economy

Laredo's customs brokers, freight forwarders, trucking carriers, and distribution centers run sizable hourly workforces, and turnover in logistics and warehousing is a constant challenge. A stronger benefits menu helps an employer hold onto drivers, dock workers, and clerical staff in a tight border labor market. Healthcare providers, retailers, and the school districts and college campuses that serve a growing population face the same pressure to recruit and retain. ## Benefits at scale for high-headcount employers

Many Laredo employers run large hourly rosters in trade, logistics, and warehousing, which is exactly where a cafeteria plan reaches the most people. A distribution center or carrier with hundreds of workers can extend pre-tax benefits across its whole roster at once, but those benefits only land if employees actually enroll. Participation depends on employee education, and that is where administration matters. Benefit Builder runs the education that keeps participation strong, explaining the plan in plain terms so workers understand what they are electing and why their paycheck changes. As a routine matter, those pre-tax elections also reduce the wages subject to payroll tax, which modestly lowers an employer's payroll cost per enrolled worker, but the draw for a high-headcount Laredo employer is putting a stronger benefit in front of a large workforce.
